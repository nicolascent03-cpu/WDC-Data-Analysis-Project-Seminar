      About the data

      How the numbers are built

      Three public datasets, joined into one table of country-years from 1950 to 2025. Here is how they are combined, what the main finding measures, and where to be careful.

      Prototype status

      This page is a working prototype of the Streamlit app. The figures come from a test run of the project notebooks on the real downloads. Before quoting any number, check it against your own notebook results.

      Pipeline

      • One country key (V-Dem country code) for all three sources; UCDP location codes match V-Dem COW codes, 31 SIPRI names are matched by hand.
      • UCDP rows with several locations are split into one row per country; conflict-years are merged into periods for the bars.
      • SIPRI deliveries without a delivery year and non-state recipients are removed; imports are summed per country and year.
      • V-Dem defines which country exists in which year. Years without a SIPRI delivery count as 0, years without a UCDP conflict as no conflict.
      • The world map also has an Arms flows mode: SIPRI deliveries drawn from supplier to recipient for the chosen year. Only the largest flows of each year are drawn, so smaller deliveries are left out.

      Main finding

      • Onset: a year with an active conflict after a year without one.
      • Arms group: mean imports in the five years before onset, split at the median of all onsets.
      • Change: democracy index minus its value in the year before onset, averaged per group and year.
      • The chart shows patterns. Conflict, arms imports and regime change influence each other, so it shows no causes.

      Caveats

      • SIPRI TIV measures the military capability transferred. It is not a price.
      • UCDP counts a conflict only from 25 battle deaths per year.
      • V-Dem scores are expert assessments; small changes from one year to the next carry little meaning.
      • The map uses today's borders. Historical states such as East Germany or South Yemen are only in the search.
      • Some states appear under a successor's code, as in V-Dem: Russia stands for the Soviet Union before 1991, Czechia for Czechoslovakia, Serbia for Yugoslavia, Germany for West Germany before 1990, Yemen for North Yemen before 1990 and Vietnam for North Vietnam before 1976. Country groups such as the Warsaw Pact or the UN Security Council use these codes.
